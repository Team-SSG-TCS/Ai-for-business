ABSTRACT 
 
Choosing a suitable technology career and identifying the skills required to reach it 
posed a challenge for students with uneven exposure to programming languages, tools 
and applied concepts. Existing career guidance and learning platforms often addressed 
role selection and content delivery separately. This project developed CareerCompass 
AI as a prototype connecting technical skill-profile analysis with a structured learning 
workspace. The career-analysis component accepted technical-skill text, normalized 
the input, converted it into Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency features and 
applied a multi-class Logistic Regression classifier to rank likely roles. It returned up 
to three candidate roles, confidence values, matched and missing skill tokens, and a 
token-based coverage percentage. The curated dataset contained 440 labelled skill 
profiles across 11 technology roles, with 40 profiles per role. The repository-reported 
five-fold stratified cross-validation benchmark showed 78.86% mean accuracy with a 
standard deviation of 1.54%; macro precision, recall and F1-score were 0.8014, 
0.7886 and 0.7833. The learning component requested a six-to-eight-node prerequisite 
graph from an external generative service and supported concept explanations, 
practice tasks, learning resources, prerequisite-based unlocking, completion tracking, 
recommendations and progress visualizations. The resource workflow searched 
educational videos, retrieved available transcripts and selected a short segment; 
submitted practice answers could receive automated scores and feedback. The results 
demonstrated the feasibility of connecting role hypotheses, vocabulary-based gap 
summaries and AI-assisted learning functions. They did not establish live 
labour-market demand or employment outcomes. The skill-role dataset remained 
curated and static, the coverage measure represented vocabulary overlap rather than 
proficiency, and a stricter evaluation was needed because vectorization preceded the 
documented cross-validation folds. The prototype was therefore positioned as 
educational decision support. Live job-posting aggregation, integrated end-to-end 
testing and user-centred evaluation remained future work. 
Keywords: Career recommendation; skill gap analysis; text classification; knowledge graph; 
personalized learning path.

APPENDIX IV 
INDIVIDUAL WORK CONTRIBUTION 
PROJECT: DYNAMIC EVENT TICKETING MARKETPLACE WITH PREDICTIVE 
SCALPING MITIGATION ALGORITHMS  
DEEPAK A (7376242AD136) 
•​ Deepak built the TicketFlow back end in Java 17 and Spring Boot 3.5.6. He 
developed the REST APIs for login (JWT access and refresh tokens), event 
management, seat setup, reservations and booking confirmation. He also built the 
real-time updates using Server-Sent Events (SSE). These send live queue position, 
wait time and seat status to users . 
•​ He implemented the anti-bot and fair-access features. The virtual queue uses Redis 
lists with normal and VIP lanes. The QueueDecisionService switches between 
direct access and a waiting queue based on system load. Admitted users get a 
temporary X-Queue-Token that is invalidated after booking. He also added the 
bot-detection checks and the ticket limits that block bulk and automated booking 
attempt . 
•​ He made booking safe under heavy traffic. Redis distributed seat locks, sorted to 
avoid deadlocks, and Hibernate @Version optimistic locking prevent double 
booking. An Idempotency-Key header stops duplicate charges. Quartz jobs open 
and close sales on schedule. RabbitMQ sends confirmation emails with a 
dead-letter queue and retries, and Actuator with Prometheus monitors the system .
